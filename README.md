Note: Valued resources are defined as things that have value and are subject to allocation/exchange. Valuation is what allows a resource to be recognized, after which it can be compared and differentiated from others, establishing its relative worth. This enables resources to be compared, allocated, exchanged, and managed within their environment.


 What actually defines an economic system? Most textbooks claim it's entirely based on scarcity managing limited supplies against unlimited wants. But imagine a hypothetical society where a primary physical resource is practically infinite. And freely available to everyone. Even without competition for that resource, individuals still possess diverse unique skills and they naturally assign different relative worth to those specific talents. If nobody has to compete traditional economics suggests the entire system should just stop. But instead people naturally begin trading their specialized services directly with each other. This reveals theorist lucian monroe's core definition. An economy is not defined by its limits. It is fundamentally the dynamic interaction and exchange of valued resources within an environment. Scarcity is simply one possible boundary condition placed on that system. It's an environmental factor. Not the reason the network exists in the first place. So even in a hypothetical world where physical resources become completely infinite and freely available to everyone, the continuous dynamic exchange of our diverse human skills. Ensures that a functioning economy never actually stops.
